# Procedural

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Prose applies this transform within the `procedural` override scope assigned by the Writing Style Map. It expresses established actions as instructions the reader can follow, with clear operating conditions and recognizable results. The Prose Rewrite Constraints and the Map's directives continue to apply.

## 2. Writing Rules

### 2.1 Actions and Operational Context

**Direct instructions.** Use imperative verb forms. State the action and its object directly. When the reader needs an operating context, identify the relevant tool, location, or environment before the action, using information already present in the Draft.

**Instruction boundaries.** Keep independent instructions easy to distinguish. Prefer separate sentences when combining actions would obscure what the reader must do. Closely related actions can share a sentence when their relationship remains clear. Sentence boundaries need not become new steps or Semantic Units.

Use statements for system behavior, results, and explanations. These support the procedure without becoming commands.

**Before:**

> Click File > New > Document in Google Docs.

**After:**

> In Google Docs, click File > New > Document.

### 2.2 Conditions and Action Status

**Conditions.** Within a sentence, place the applicable condition before the command it governs. Keep each condition attached to the correct action, including conditions that determine an existing branch. This sentence-level choice does not change the established order of Semantic Units.

**Action status.** Make required, recommended, optional, alternative, and prohibited actions distinguishable. Preserve their original force: a recommendation remains a recommendation, and an optional action remains optional. Use explicit wording when a modal would leave that distinction unclear.

**Before:**

> Read the log if the build fails.

**After:**

> If the build fails, read the log.

### 2.3 Results, Warnings, and Supporting Information

**Results.** Distinguish the reader's action from the system's response. Express established outcomes clearly enough to help the reader recognize progress, completion, or the applicable next branch. Preserve whether an outcome is expected, possible, or already observed.

**Warnings.** Make the guarded action or condition and its consequence explicit. In a warning, lead with the command or condition and then explain the risk. Preserve its connection to the affected action within the established Presentation Order.

**Supporting information.** Use notes for supplementary information the reader can skip and still complete the task. Required actions, prerequisites, and information needed to recognize completion belong in the procedural text. Keep explanations in statement form and retain their connection to the actions they support.

**Before:**

> Caution: The reset erases saved profiles, so do not reset the device before exporting them.

**After:**

> Caution: Do not reset the device before exporting saved profiles. The reset erases them.
