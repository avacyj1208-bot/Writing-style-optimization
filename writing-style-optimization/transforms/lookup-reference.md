# Lookup Reference

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Prose applies this transform within the `lookup-reference` override scope assigned by the Writing Style Map. It expresses established entries so readers can look up a named item and understand its meaning, properties, or behavior without reading the document continuously. The Prose Rewrite Constraints and the Map's directives continue to apply.

Work within the existing entry boundaries, grouping, Presentation Order, and assigned Surface Realization.

## 2. Writing Rules

### 2.1 Direct Descriptions

**Meaning and behavior.** Describe what an entry denotes or what the referenced object does. Use factual descriptions rather than instructions addressed to the reader or evaluations of the object's importance.

**Opening description.** Begin with useful meaning that the entry's name does not already convey. Remove introductions that merely announce the description or repeat the name. A concept may need a defining phrase; a function may need a statement of behavior. Choose the form that directly expresses the established content.

**Before:**

> **Renewable resource**
>
> The term renewable resource is used to describe a resource that natural processes can replace on a timescale relevant to its use. It is important to note that renewable does not mean that the resource is unlimited, because the amount available can still decline when people use it faster than it is replaced. Sunlight is continuously available, while forests and freshwater depend on biological or hydrological replenishment and therefore recover at different rates.

**After:**

> **Renewable resource**
>
> A resource replenished by natural processes on a timescale relevant to its use. Renewable resources are not necessarily unlimited: their available stock can decline when consumption exceeds replenishment. Sunlight is continuously available, whereas forests and freshwater recover through biological or hydrological processes at different rates.

### 2.2 Local Interpretability

**Explicit referents.** Use identifiable object names where a pronoun or phrase such as *the previous option* would require reading another entry to identify its referent. Retain repetition that makes selective reading possible. Cross-references should name their targets or use existing links rather than rely on relative position alone.

**Conditions and values.** Keep each condition, unit, range, and special value attached to the property or behavior it qualifies. Make distinctions such as a default versus an allowed range, or an omitted value versus an explicit setting, clear wherever they occur in the Draft.

An entry may rely on its current heading, column headings, and clearly applicable shared explanations. Resolve references from established context without repeating all background in every entry or importing content from other entries.

**Before:**

> Admission categories apply to one museum visit. Children under five enter free.
>
> **Standard admission**
>
> This category is for visitors from 18 through 64 years of age. It includes entry to all permanent galleries. Special exhibitions require a separate ticket.
>
> **Student admission**
>
> This is available to full-time students who are 18 or older and show current student identification when they arrive. It provides the same gallery access as the category above, but the reduction does not apply to the separate ticket mentioned there.
>
> **Family admission**
>
> This covers as many as two adults and three children from 5 through 17 years of age. They receive the same access as standard visitors. Additional adults or children need the corresponding individual admission, and this category also does not include special exhibitions.

**After:**

> Admission categories apply to one museum visit. Children under five enter free.
>
> **Standard admission**
>
> For visitors aged 18–64. Includes all permanent galleries; special exhibitions require a separate ticket.
>
> **Student admission**
>
> For full-time students aged 18 or older. Current student identification is required at entry. Student admission includes all permanent galleries. The student reduction does not apply to separate special-exhibition tickets.
>
> **Family admission**
>
> For up to two adults and three children aged 5–17. Family admission includes all permanent galleries; special exhibitions require separate tickets. Additional visitors need the corresponding individual admission.

### 2.3 Parallel and Compact Wording

**Parallel descriptions.** Use comparable grammatical forms and stable wording for entries that express comparable properties. Let substantive differences stand out without making the reader repeatedly interpret a new phrasing of the same property. Different functions may need different forms; consistency does not require identical sentence patterns or lengths.

**Compact expression.** Where the assigned heading, label, or column heading supplies sufficient context, use an interpretable phrase instead of repeating a full introduction. Retain complete sentences where conditions, exceptions, or complex relationships need them. Across a group of entries, remove repeated introductory wording while preserving each entry's distinguishing information and necessary qualifications.

**Before:**

> **Full sun**
>
> Plants in this category generally need a location that receives direct sunlight for at least six hours each day. The direct light can occur at any time of day, although some plants in this group may still benefit from protection during periods of extreme afternoon heat.
>
> **Partial shade**
>
> This category describes plants that usually grow best with three to six hours of direct sunlight per day. In hot climates, they often do better when the direct light comes in the morning and they are protected from strong afternoon sun.
>
> **Full shade**
>
> When a plant is placed in this category, it generally needs less than three hours of direct sunlight each day. Full shade does not mean complete darkness: these plants still require indirect or filtered light.

**After:**

> **Full sun**
>
> Generally requires at least six hours of direct sunlight per day. Some plants may benefit from protection during extreme afternoon heat.
>
> **Partial shade**
>
> Generally requires three to six hours of direct sunlight per day. In hot climates, morning sun and afternoon protection are often preferable.
>
> **Full shade**
>
> Generally requires fewer than three hours of direct sunlight per day. Full shade still requires indirect or filtered light; it does not mean complete darkness.
